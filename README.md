## Current Research

### Sustainable Carbon Materials for Energy Storage

I am investigating the design, synthesis, characterization, and electrochemical performance of biomass-derived activated carbon electrodes for sustainable supercapacitor applications.

My research explores renewable carbon precursors including:

- Spent coffee grounds
- Coconut husk

with an emphasis on understanding relationships between:

Processing, Structure, Surface Chemistry, Porosity, Electrochemical Performance

### Emerging Research Direction: Electrochemical CO₂ Reduction

I have recently begun exploring electrochemical CO₂ reduction as an emerging research direction, with interest in how catalytic materials and electrochemical processes can be used to convert captured CO₂ into useful products.

My current exploration is focused on understanding:

- Catalyst design for electrochemical CO₂ reduction
- Electrode and catalyst-material interactions
- Reaction pathways and product selectivity
- Electrochemical conversion efficiency
- Carbon-based and hybrid catalytic materials
- The possibility of coupling CO₂ capture and electrochemical utilization
- Integrated systems that can move from capture, activation and conversion

I am particularly interested in the long-term possibility of developing or contributing to integrated CO₂ capture and utilization systems, where materials are designed not only to adsorb or capture carbon dioxide, but also to support its subsequent electrochemical conversion into value-added products.

This is a developing area of my research, and I am currently building deeper expertise in the catalytic, electrochemical, and materials-design principles required for this field.
